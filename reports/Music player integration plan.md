# Music player integration plan

Research and planning only · 15 November 2026

**Build one richer music player around our existing native engine.** BitChord offers valuable library, queue, lyrics, artwork and offline-collection ideas. StaaaaandBy offers a distinctive fullscreen artwork-and-lyrics display. Together, they can make our app feel like a personal listening room while retaining its identity as a precise hi-res workstation. The recommended first delivery is a complete local listening experience: **open a real album → arrange its queue → play through our native engine → read its lyrics → enter Dock Mode → stop after the track**. Keep the five tabs and existing DAP skins. Add capabilities through shared data and presentation layers, rather than combining three playback systems. This document proposes the work; no application source was changed and no build or test was run.

**1. The product to aim for**

Keep **Playing, Library, Path, DSP and Output** in their current order. Playing gains an expanded lyrics view and a fullscreen Dock entry. Library gains stronger album/artist/folder browsing, playlists, favorites and offline collections. Path explains what the engine knows about the source, processing and actual output. DSP and Output retain their existing responsibilities. Existing Liquid, Deck and Afterglow presentations remain available; dock becomes another presentation of the same live session. The latest app already has the relevant fullscreen DAP entry and state repository, so this direction extends an established design. ([Existing DAP screen](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/ui/DapModeScreen.kt:73), [state repository](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/state/AudioEngineStateRepository.kt:38))

The following are **proposed experiences**, not claims about current functionality. They give the integration a coherent purpose and help avoid accumulating unrelated donor features.

| Experience | What the user sees and does | Why it belongs | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Album Room** | Fullscreen sleeve artwork, a progress-driven slit of color, a warm clock, optional lyrics and clear playback controls. Portrait and landscape layouts share the current queue. | Combines StaaaaandBy's strongest visual idea with our DAP identity. It can work completely offline. | First release |
| **Edition Shelf** | Different releases, masters and source copies appear as understandable versions. The user can choose the exact recording rather than silently receiving another remaster or lossy replacement. | A hi-res library should preserve music identity as carefully as sample format. | Data model first; richer presentation later |
| **Travel Crate** | Mark an album or playlist for offline use; see separate readiness for audio, artwork and lyrics, with actual size and verification state. | Turns downloading into a reliable collection feature rather than a pile of files. | After durable source/download contracts |
| **Reference, Studio and Night Dock scenes** | Reference favors untouched playback; Studio exposes the chosen native processing; Night Dock uses a calm, dim presentation and sleep controls. | Makes the product approachable without hiding what changes the sound. A visual scene must never silently change gain, resampling or DSP. | Presentation first; explicit saved audio settings later |
| **Finish this album, then sleep** | A timer follows queue/album boundaries instead of guessing remaining time from one track's duration. | Particularly useful for album listening and a bedside dock. | After ordinary sleep-after-track |

Album Room should have three carefully chosen appearances: **Gallery** for artwork, **Reading Room** for calm native lyrics, and optional **Lyric Theatre** for animated typography. Keep restrained art-derived colors inside the current visual system, with readable contrast and a static/reduced-motion alternative. Typography, clock and transport controls should remain recognizable across all three. Invisible tap thirds can be an optional shortcut; visible controls and accessibility semantics must remain available. StaaaaandBy's slit-scan and JIZURA renderer both consume artwork or timing information without needing the audio stream. ([Slit-scan implementation](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/StaaaaandBy/app/src/main/java/com/kazuto/standby/ui/StandbyScreen.kt:388), [lyric renderer contract](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/StaaaaandBy/app/src/main/assets/lyric/lyric.js:229))

**2. What to bring over, adapt or leave for later**

BitChord is a broad music ecosystem, with Android/shared UI, web, TV, Wear and a party backend. Its large playback service combines source resolution, ExoPlayer construction, transitions, session management and other concerns. It is a useful behavioral reference, but importing that service would entangle our native engine with a second playback architecture. StaaaaandBy is smaller and more focused, but much of its complexity exists because it observes other apps. Our own player already knows its track, queue and position. ([BitChord service](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/BitChord/app/src/main/java/com/music/bitchord/playback/PlaybackService.kt:312), [our native player](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/playback/NativeEnginePlayer.kt:45))

| Donor capability | Recommended treatment | Concrete integration |
|---|---|---|
| BitChord album, artist and folder browsing | **Adapt** | Extend our library with reliable disc/track ordering, compilation handling, search and explicit include/exclude rules. Preserve short interludes and classical movements. |
| Playlists, favorites and history | **Implement around our models** | Store ordered references and migrations in a unified database; avoid importing parallel preference-based stores. |
| Queue editing and stable shuffle | **Extend what exists** | Add missing reorder/play-next/add-last/restore behavior to our existing queue. Reject stale asynchronous preparation after an edit. |
| Line/word lyrics and timing correction | **Adapt as one shared feature** | Embedded/imported lyrics first, native reader, manual offset and real timing granularity. Add permitted online providers individually. |
| Album artwork, color and animation | **Adapt selectively** | Embedded/local artwork first; restrained palette changes and optional licensed/local motion assets. |
| Sleep timer | **Implement early** | Monotonic deadline plus a real track-end event. Define skip, repeat, queue-end and process-recreation behavior. |
| Downloads and network quality controls | **Adapt mechanics** | Durable jobs, staging, verification, cancellation, retry and separate streaming/download policies for supported sources. |
| StaaaaandBy slit-scan/clock layout | **Adapt early** | Manual native Dock Mode using our player state, with portrait/landscape support. |
| StaaaaandBy JIZURA typography | **Optional later renderer** | Isolated visual module over the same lyric document and clock; native fallback always available. |
| External music-app watcher and Spotify hybrid polling | **Omit initially** | Unnecessary for displaying our own music; avoids stale-session heuristics and extra permissions. |
| Automatic lock-screen charging launch | **Research and prototype later** | Keep manual entry reliable first. Evaluate system screensaver support on S24+/One UI. |
| BitChord DSP and USB engine | **Reference only** | Preserve our native pipeline and validate changes under its existing phase gates. |
| Automix, speed and silence skipping | **Separate processed-listening scope** | Extend native behavior only when justified; clearly show that samples are changed. |
| NAS, scrobbling, playlist imports | **Later additions** | Finish local/Drive/Telegram foundations first; use explicit account and privacy controls. |
| Cast, party rooms, TV, Wear and web | **Separate roadmap** | Each adds another device, transport or service lifecycle; none is required for the first phone integration. |

The existing app already implements **queue UI, crossfade settings, three DAP skins, native DSP/USB source and telemetry**. These need delta reviews and validation, not duplicate implementations. Crossfade defaults off and has album-related settings, but the native player's current same-album decision does not fully match the offered preference. Resolve that policy before considering DJ-style transitions. ([Queue sheet](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/ui/QueueSheet.kt), [transition settings](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/playback/TransitionSettings.kt:24), [preload policy](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/playback/NativeEnginePlayer.kt:490))

The donor audio engines have material compatibility differences. BitChord's DSP uses Float32 and a different stage order; its USB descriptor planner rejects implicit feedback, which our requirements include. Its USB worker uses blocking-lock and exception mechanisms that conflict with our pump rules. These findings justify studying behavior and failure handling while retaining our own engine architecture. They do not establish that either app works correctly on our DACs. ([Donor DSP](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/BitChord/app/src/main/java/com/music/bitchord/playback/audio/DspChain.kt:57), [implicit-feedback rejection](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/BitChord/app/src/main/java/com/music/bitchord/playback/audio/bitperfect/usb/UsbDescriptors.kt:281), [USB worker](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/BitChord/app/src/main/cpp/bitperfect/usb_transport.cpp:273))

The code-reuse decision can wait. **BitChord's main code license is GPLv3:** copying covered application code into a distributed combined app generally brings corresponding-source and GPL-compatible distribution obligations. It does not prohibit selling the app. Some files have separate Apache/LGPL provenance, which requires component-level review. **StaaaaandBy is MIT**, with additional JIZURA and font notices that must be retained. The planning assumption is to design our own implementations of selected BitChord capabilities and evaluate permissively licensed components at their upstream sources. A superficial rewrite of copied code is not a licensing solution. No license choice or donor code import is needed to review this plan. ([BitChord license](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/BitChord/LICENSE), [GNU combination guidance](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html#WhatDoesCompatMean), [StaaaaandBy license](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/StaaaaandBy/LICENSE), [bundled notices](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/StaaaaandBy/licenses/JIZURA-MIT.txt))

**3. Share the foundations so each feature improves the others**

The architecture should have **one library identity, one queue owner, one playback engine and one playback clock**. Our existing Media3 session supplies system controls; Playing, lyrics, dock, notifications and later companions consume that same authority. Artwork and lyric rendering never own another player or pass PCM through a WebView. The current service already provides a single native-backed player/session seam. ([Service](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/service/DeterministicAudioService.kt:53))

Use a stable internal track ID, a separate recording/release identity, and explicit source versions. A local file and a Drive copy can represent the same selected version without making a YouTube ID or expiring URL the canonical identity. Title alone must not merge live recordings, remasters, clean/explicit versions or unrelated albums. Every queue occurrence also needs its own entry ID: the same track can appear twice. Queue edits must invalidate affected prepared-next audio, artwork and lyric work. Persist the current entry, ordering, shuffle state and position with schema migrations. BitChord's pure queue rules and generation-aware edits demonstrate the need; our existing queue remains the owner. ([Donor queue model](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/BitChord/shared/src/jvmSharedMain/kotlin/com/music/bitchord/playback/QueueWindow.kt:34), [generation-aware edits](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/BitChord/app/src/main/java/com/music/bitchord/playback/BoundedQueueEdits.kt:11))

Separate **catalogue discovery** from **opening audio bytes**. A provider can offer metadata without granting downloading, native decoding or exact output. Opening a selected source should resolve to a local descriptor or our seekable remote-byte interface, with cancellation, authentication refresh, ownership and capability information. File/network/database/artwork work stays off the audio callback and USB pump. Prefer the exact selected source version; fallback to another rendition must be visible and controlled. The current remote-source interface already places blocking reads on decoder threads and has native callback plumbing. ([Remote contract](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/library/source/RemoteByteSource.kt:5), [native byte-source bridge](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/cpp/jni/KotlinByteSource.cpp))

Lyrics need one immutable document carrying text, true plain/line/word timing, source attribution, track/version identity, user offset and cache metadata. Resolve embedded or imported local text first, then cache, then an optional eligible network provider. Keep loading, genuine absence, instrumental, offline, rate-limited and failed states separate. A temporary timeout must not become a week-long “no lyrics” result, as can happen in the donor cache path. Keep artwork visible during lookup and discard results belonging to a previous track. Never invent word-level accuracy from line-only timestamps. ([BitChord lyric repository](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/BitChord/shared/src/jvmSharedMain/kotlin/com/music/bitchord/data/lyrics/LyricsRepository.kt:87), [StaaaaandBy error/miss path](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/StaaaaandBy/app/src/main/java/com/kazuto/standby/lyrics/LyricsRepository.kt:130))

The display clock should publish track/queue identity, actual source position, monotonic timestamp, playback speed and playing/buffering state. Renderers can extrapolate smooth motion between updates, resetting on pause, seek, transition and speed changes. Do not query JNI every display frame. During crossfade, define exactly when lyric/art identity transfers to the next track; animation must follow the same rule as queue state. Unknown output latency remains unknown rather than a fabricated correction.

Dock should begin as a manually opened fullscreen presentation, without notification-listener access, overlay access, a boot watcher or a second persistent service. Automatic charging/locked-screen behavior is a later platform feature: Android restricts background activity launches, and a foreground service alone does not grant unrestricted fullscreen launch. `DreamService` is a relevant official screensaver route, but its discoverability and behavior must be checked on the actual S24+/One UI combination. Do not describe a normal app display as Samsung's system AOD. ([Background launch rules](https://developer.android.com/guide/components/activities/secure-bal), [DreamService](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/service/dreams/DreamService))

For optional lyric theatre, restrict local WebView content/origins, cap artwork and lyric sizes, bundle chosen fonts with notices, use a readiness handshake and fall back to native lyrics after a script error. Pause or release visual work when hidden, dimmed or stopped. Test the combined load of charging, USB playback, DSP and animation. Offer reduced motion, large text, clock-only privacy and deliberate control locking. Pixel movement and dimming can reduce static exposure; they are not a guarantee against OLED burn-in. **Reduce visual workload before compromising audio quality.**

Online content needs provider-specific treatment. LRCLIB's API documents identification and backoff behavior, but an open-source server license is not a blanket commercial grant for lyric text. Keep the first lyric experience useful with embedded and user-imported files. Spotify's policy restricts synchronization of recordings with visual media and mixing, so do not inherit the donor's Spotify-plus-lyric-video combination as a release feature. Apple/Spotify integrations can begin with permitted metadata/playlist matching; protected playback stays in official SDK/player paths, with native DSP/exact-output limitations disclosed. YouTube extraction/download behavior and Discord ordinary-account automation should not be inherited as assumed Play-ready features. ([LRCLIB API](https://lrclib.net/docs.txt), [Spotify policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy), [YouTube policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies), [Discord account automation policy](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002192352-Automated-User-Accounts-Self-Bots))

**4. Implement in product slices with measurable exits**

These packages sit beneath the approved engine phases; they do not replace their CI and S24+ gates. Relative effort describes scope, not a promised schedule. Establish the baseline before assigning calendar estimates.

| Order | Delivery and relative scope | Acceptance criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Establish the baseline** | Preserve the newer D: app snapshot, reconcile it with the older chat checkout, record current features and dependencies, confirm existing engine gates. Small planning effort; native remediation can be large. | Source revision is known; existing work is preserved; baseline test results are recorded when implementation starts; output claims distinguish requested, active and verified states. |
| **B — Complete the local listening slice** | Extend existing library/queue contracts, persistent ordering and source identity; add embedded/imported lyrics, native lyric reader, manual dock art/clock and sleep-after-track. Medium-to-large integration. | Real local album plays through the same native session; queue edits survive restart; lyrics stay correct through pause/seek/skip; dock rotation/exit does not reopen audio; sleep follows a real end event. |
| **C — Polish the collection** | Albums/artists/folders/search, favorites/playlists/recent history, Edition Shelf foundations and richer queue actions. Medium. | Disc/track order is stable; same-title releases remain separate; duplicate queue entries work; migrations retain data; unavailable sources have useful states. |
| **D — Make offline/cloud reliable** | Durable authorized downloads, Travel Crate, pinned storage vs disposable cache, source health and policy. Finish Drive auth/pagination/range playback, then TDLib within its engine phase. Large, staged per source. | Expired credentials, interrupted writes, ignored Range requests, low storage and process death recover safely; incomplete files are never published; pinned media survives cache eviction. |
| **E — Add optional visual theatre** | Curated JIZURA, local fonts, shared lyric document, performance settings, privacy and night behavior. Medium-to-large. | Native fallback works offline; no stale lyrics or arbitrary navigation; accessibility and reduced motion remain usable; sustained S24+ measurements show acceptable visual load and no audio regression. |
| **F — Evaluate automatic dock and extras** | DreamService prototype, optional NAS/scrobbling/imports; companions/social/Automix separately scoped. High uncertainty for automatic lock behavior. | Each feature passes its platform, credential, privacy and audio gates; manual dock remains available if automation is unsupported. |

Package B is deliberately a complete experience rather than several isolated screens. It exercises shared identity, queue state, clock, artwork/lyrics and lifecycle together, while avoiding provider availability as a dependency. It should produce an app that is visibly better even before online discovery, external accounts or animation are added.

The source review identified several baseline items that affect the plan. **These are code observations, not reproduced device failures.** Drive's current UI fully prefetches the selected file before native playback even though range-source helpers exist; token refresh and listing pagination also need completion. Telegram exposes an adapter interface without a complete inspected TDLib account/client flow. The seek cache uses SQLiteOpenHelper rather than the approved Room design, so introduce migrations rather than destructive replacement. ([Drive playback flow](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/library/LibraryScreen.kt:473), [Drive client](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/library/drive/GoogleDriveClient.kt:43), [Telegram adapter](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/library/source/TelegramByteSource.kt:12), [cache upgrade](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/library/cache/SeekTableCache.kt:165))

Some output grades are inferred from the selected route when an actual receipt grade is absent. Render-buffer hashes also do not independently measure the external DAC's received bytes or analog output. Separate internal sample equality, OS-confirmed routing and independently measured endpoint behavior. Android's bit-perfect mixer capability is optional, so query support per phone/DAC/firmware. Crossfade and enabled processing change samples and must not carry an untouched bit-exact label. ([Grade fallback](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/kotlin/com/audiophile/engine/model/SignalPathState.kt:483), [render hashes](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/cpp/core/PlaybackEngine.cpp:1682), [AOSP mixer capability](https://source.android.com/docs/core/audio/preferred-mixer-attr))

Native remediation also needs its own work: the inspected render chain stores intermediate samples as Float32 despite the approved Float64 design, and its Vulkan convolution path reaches a synchronous fence wait with a 20 ms timeout. That call path is a deterministic-render concern; this review did not measure actual underruns. Keep visual additions independent of resolving those engine issues, and do not advertise unvalidated precision or latency guarantees. ([Render chain](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/cpp/core/PlaybackEngine.cpp:1667), [Vulkan wait](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/src/main/cpp/dsp/VulkanConvolver.cpp:819))

Before release, update the API34 baseline against current Play target requirements and verify **every packaged native library** plus APK/AAB alignment for 16 KB pages; one linker flag does not validate FFmpeg, libsoxr, libc++ and packaging together. Inspect the merged manifest for network permissions and execute Kotlin tests/lint explicitly alongside the existing native suite. Recheck live Play requirements when preparing release rather than freezing policy deadlines into this feature plan. ([App build](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/src/app/build.gradle.kts:8), [target API requirements](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk), [16 KB guide](https://developer.android.com/guide/practices/page-sizes), [current CI](/D:/MusicPlayer-Projects/Music-Player/.github/workflows/android.yml))

**5. What completion should mean**

| Area | Required evidence during implementation |
|---|---|
| Audio integrity | Exact-bypass checks for supported formats; same-format gapless and changed-format reopen behavior; honest processing status; no extra underruns when lyrics/dock start or stop. |
| Hardware | S24+ with Snowsky Echo and representative UAC2/DSP/Bluetooth routes; plug/unplug, denied routes, fallback, explicit/implicit feedback where required; claims match what was actually measured. |
| Queue and library | Persistence, migration, reordered prepared-next item, repeated track occurrences, stable shuffle, unavailable URIs and distinct editions. |
| Lyrics | Offset round-trip, repeated timestamps, malformed/huge files, plain/instrumental/missing states, long tracks, Unicode/RTL, pause/seek/skip races, real word timing, offline and provider backoff. |
| Dock and theatre | Both orientations, configuration/process recreation, visible exit, side key, background/screen-off cleanup, font scaling/TalkBack/reduced motion, privacy, sustained charging-plus-playback heat and power comparison. |
| Sources/downloads | OAuth refresh/revocation, cancellation, head/tail indexing, range seeks, interrupted/resumed jobs, checksum mismatch, low storage and pinned-file protection. |
| Release | Existing engine phase gates, native sanitizers, Kotlin tests and lint, packaged decoder capabilities, merged manifest, target API/16 KB validation and component/data notices. |

The review covered the donors' major source modules, contracts, entry points and representative tests, plus the target's playback/state/library/remote/render/UI paths. The four required PDFs total 117 pages and were reviewed for architecture themes and relevant RT/USB/DSP passages. Some older reference claims conflict with the approved rules, including FIFO scheduling, protected-audio DSP routing and affinity guidance; the approved rules take precedence. This was **not** a line-by-line audit of all 1,409 BitChord files, every JIZURA animation, every DSP equation or all native code. No login, build, benchmark, DAC null test or physical S24+ verification was performed. Source presence establishes an implementation to study, not a passed release gate.

The next implementation specification should lock the first local listening slice, its data/command contracts and its acceptance fixtures. That creates a stable base for either a quiet reference player or a visually rich listening room, with the same library and playback authority underneath. Broader cloud and companion features can then earn their place through demonstrated value rather than arriving automatically with a donor repository.
